title: Tasks & areas
description: Six status views, one composer, and labels that cut across everything. A task is an issue — nothing more to learn.
---

A task is one GitHub issue. Its title is the issue title, its notes are the
issue body, and completing it closes the issue. Everything below is a way of
looking at that one fact.

## The six views

| View | What belongs there |
| --- | --- |
| **Inbox** | Anything captured without a decision yet. |
| **Today** | Due today, or picked by you for today. |
| **Upcoming** | Has a due date, later. |
| **Anytime** | Ready whenever you are. No date. |
| **Someday** | Maybe. Out of sight, not out of reach. |
| **Logbook** | Done. Closed issues, newest first. |

The view a task lands in follows its due date. Give a task a date and it moves
on its own; clear the date and it returns to Anytime.

## Capture

The composer sits at the top of every list. Type, press <kbd>↵</kbd>, keep
going. The task appears immediately and syncs to GitHub in the background — a
row dimmed with a soft pulse is still in flight, and a red cloud marker means
the write failed and will be retried.

:::note
An optimistic row is never a lie. If GitHub refuses the write, the row rolls
back and tells you — see [Troubleshooting](/troubleshooting/).
:::

## Areas

An area is a GitHub label, and it cuts across every view: **Work**,
**Personal**, **Health**, **Finance**, **Learning**. Pick one on a task, or
filter a list by it with the chips under the page title.

Areas are a *facet* — a task has one, and it travels with it everywhere.
[Projects](/projects/) are a *place*. That is the whole difference.

## Due dates and overdue

A due date can carry a time. When a task is past due and still open, its row
takes a red edge, its title turns red, and the date is replaced by a single
**Overdue** label — one clear signal instead of three.

## Completing a task

Click the circle, or press <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>↵</kbd> on the focused row. The
task closes on GitHub and appears in your Logbook. Nothing is deleted, and the
history stays readable in the repository forever.

:::tip[Your logbook isn't a feature we built]
It's git history you could already read.
:::
